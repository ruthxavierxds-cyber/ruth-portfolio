# Local Quest — Same-Day Cash Listing Package
**For:** Ruth (creative-technologist, WSL/Windows, bilingual EN/PT)
**Goal:** Earn same-day cash from neighbors with zero specialized skills — just showing up, being honest, and doing the boring stuff well.
**Rule of thumb:** Honest about being a beginner. Confident about delivering. Never oversell.

## 2. Five concrete service ideas (suited to Ruth)

### A. Tech help for non-tech neighbors (1-on-1, in-home or remote)
- Set up a new phone/tablet, transfer photos, install apps, connect printer/Wi-Fi, create email accounts, back up photos to cloud.
- "Make my phone less confusing" session: organize apps, set large text, turn on spam call blocking, teach 3 things they'll actually use.
- Smart TV / streaming setup (Netflix, YouTube, casting from phone).
- **Why it fits Ruth:** She lives in WSL/Windows daily; patient with beginners; bilingual means she can help PT-speaking neighbors who get ignored by English-only techs.
- **Not in scope:** data recovery, hardware repair, "fix my hacked account" emergencies (refer out).

### B. Homework / beginner tutoring (kids or adults)
- Reading practice, basic math, English/Portuguese conversation for beginners, homework help for elementary/middle school.
- Adult "how to use a computer" lessons (email, browser, video calls, online banking basics).
- **Why it fits Ruth:** Bilingual EN/PT is rare and valuable; patient; no teaching credential needed for informal help.
- **Not in scope:** test prep guarantees, certified special-ed support, anything requiring a license.

### C. Pet care (drop-in visits + walks)
- Dog walks (30/45/60 min), cat drop-in (feed, water, litter, play), pet sitting while owner is at work.
- Photo updates sent after every visit (this alone wins repeat clients).
- **Why it fits Ruth:** Low skill, high trust, flexible hours, pairs well with errands.
- **Not in scope:** medication administration beyond simple (refer to vet tech), aggressive dogs, exotic animals.

### D. Errands & "life admin" help
- Grocery pickup, pharmacy pickup, post office, returns, waiting in line, carrying heavy bags.
- Paperwork help: filling forms, scanning, printing, organizing documents, booking appointments online.
- **Why it fits Ruth:** No skill needed, immediate cash, builds trust that leads to tech-help upsell (ethically, only when asked).
- **Not in scope:** handling cash beyond agreed amounts, legal/financial advice, driving others' cars.

### E. Simple automation help (the "noob but useful" niche)
- Set up recurring reminders, calendar events, phone alarms for meds/appointments.
- Create a simple shared grocery list or family calendar (Google Calendar/Keep/Notes).
- "One-tap" shortcuts: a single button that calls a family member, or a shortcut to a frequently used website.
- Photo organization: rename/sort phone photos into folders by year/month (manual, honest "I'll do it with you" session).
- **Why it fits Ruth:** Uses real skills she has (Python/shortcuts) but framed as plain-English help. Charge for time, not "automation consulting."
- **Not in scope:** custom software, bots, anything with passwords you don't need.

## 5. Same-day delivery checklist

**Before leaving home**
- [ ] Confirm job, address, time, price in writing (text/DM). Screenshot it.
- [ ] Charge phone to 100%; bring charger + power bank.
- [ ] Pack kit: pen, notepad, phone charger cable, a few zip bags, water bottle, snacks.
- [ ] Cash/change for errands; receipt envelope.
- [ ] Tell one trusted person where you're going and when you'll be back (share live location).
- [ ] Confirm pet: allergies? vet contact? emergency contact? house rules?

**On arrival (first 5 min)**
- [ ] Arrive 5 min early; text "I'm here" (don't knock early if they said a time).
- [ ] Greet, confirm scope + price out loud: "So today: X, for $Y, about Z minutes. Sound right?"
- [ ] Ask: "Anything else you'd like me to avoid / not touch?"
- [ ] For tech: ask for Wi-Fi password only if needed; never ask for passwords to accounts. Use "show me" not "give me."

**During the job**
- [ ] Work visibly; narrate what you're doing in plain words.
- [ ] If you hit something outside scope, stop and say: "This needs someone more skilled — I can finish X today and refer you for Y."
- [ ] Take a photo of completed work (with permission) for your own record / portfolio.
- [ ] For errands: keep every receipt; text photo of receipt before spending over $20.

**Closing (last 5 min)**
- [ ] Show the result; have them try it once while you're there.
- [ ] Collect payment (cash preferred same-day; digital if agreed).
- [ ] Ask for feedback: "Anything I could do better next time?"
- [ ] Ask referral question (see script below) — only if they're happy.
- [ ] Leave a short written summary if it was tech help (3 bullet points of what changed + "call me if X").

**After (same day)**
- [ ] Text a thank-you + "here's what we did" recap.
- [ ] Log job: date, client (first name only), service, price, duration, notes, rating.
- [ ] Update availability for tomorrow.

## 7. Safety & ethics notes

**Show up.** On time, every time. If you'll be late, text 10+ min ahead with a real ETA. Reliability is the product.

**Charge fairly.** Quote before starting. No surprise fees. If a job takes half the estimated time, charge half. If it takes double because you're slow, eat the difference — you're the one learning.

**No upselling.** Don't pitch extra services mid-job. If they ask "can you also…?", answer honestly: "Yes, that's $X and about Y minutes — want me to add it, or another day?" Let them decide.

**Scope discipline.** Stay inside what you agreed. "That's outside what I'm good at — here's someone who can help" is a complete sentence and builds more trust than faking it.

**Money handling.** Cash in a separate pocket; count it in front of them; give receipts for errands. Never "hold" their money for later.

**Personal safety.**
- First meetings in public or with a trusted person nearby when possible.
- Share live location with a trusted contact for every job.
- Trust your gut: if a request feels off, decline politely and leave. "I'm not the right fit for this" needs no explanation.
- No overnight stays at strangers' homes at first; no handling of large cash; no rides in their car alone.
- For pet jobs: meet the pet + owner together first; get vet + emergency contact in writing.

**Privacy.** Don't photograph clients' homes/screens without permission. Don't share their info. Delete their Wi-Fi passwords from your notes after the job.

**Honesty about level.** Say "I'm learning" out loud. It lowers the bar, filters out people who'd be unhappy, and makes the ones who stay loyal.

**No fabricated proof.** Don't invent testimonials, reviews, or "5-star" claims. Real first clients = real (short, specific) quotes later, with permission.

**Bilingual courtesy.** Ask which language they prefer before starting; don't switch mid-sentence to show off. For mixed-language households, agree on one for instructions.

**When to stop.** If a job makes you uncomfortable, unsafe, or dishonest — stop, refund if paid, leave. $20 is not worth it.
